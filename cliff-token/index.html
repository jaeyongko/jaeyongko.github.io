Overview

Large language models reach high accuracy in mathematical reasoning, but individual traces on the same problem diverge; some arrive at the correct answer while others fail. We introduce the cliff token, a token at which the estimated probability of reaching the correct answer drops beyond an adaptive threshold.

  1. We formalize the cliff token with a z-test-based adaptive threshold that separates statistically significant drops in success probability from sampling noise, showing that these tokens act as failure triggers.
  2. We introduce a cliff taxonomy—deterministic, uncertain, and sampled-off cliffs—showing that the proportion of each type varies across model families and sizes.
  3. We show that uncertain and sampled-off cliffs are more effective as training signals than deterministic ones under Cliff-DPO.

What Is a Cliff Token?

Success probability is the probability of reaching the correct answer conditioned on the reasoning trace generated up to a specific token position. We estimate it by running continuations from each token prefix and measuring the fraction that reach the correct answer.

A cliff token is a token where success probability drops significantly when it is generated. The trace may still be recoverable before that token; after it is fixed, continuations are more likely to fail.

We identify drops using an adaptive threshold based on a one-sided two-proportion z-test at a 95% confidence level. The base threshold is τ = 0.1, and success probability is estimated with 64 rollouts at each evaluated token position.

Do Cliff Tokens Trigger Reasoning Failures?

Across seven models and three mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM1K, MATH500, and AIME 2025), cliff tokens occur more frequently in incorrect traces than in correct traces for most models, with Llama-3.2-1B as the exception.

Resampling before the first cliff token (Cliff-del) generally yields higher pass@k than resampling after it (Cliff-keep) at the same sample count, suggesting that cliff tokens trigger reasoning failures.

Cliff Taxonomy

We characterize cliff tokens by greedy choice and token entropy. Their distribution varies by model family and scale: as model scale increases within the Qwen3 and Llama-3 families, sampled-off cliffs become less common and deterministic cliffs more common. Qwen3-8B also produces more deterministic cliffs than the similarly sized Llama-3.1-8B.

Deterministic cliff

A greedy token with entropy below 0.0561; the model samples it with near certainty.

Uncertain cliff

A greedy token with entropy at or above 0.0561; the top choice is sampled under uncertainty.

Sampled-off cliff

A non-greedy token with entropy at or above 0.0561; another choice is sampled under uncertainty.

Do Cliff Types Differ as Training Signals?

Cliff-DPO applies single-token preference optimization at identified cliff positions, pairing a non-cliff candidate token with the original cliff token. The uncertain and sampled-off variants achieve higher mean accuracy than the base model and deterministic variant across the three evaluated models on GSM8K, GSM1K, and MATH500.
